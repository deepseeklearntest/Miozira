The master list of concepts Miozira will teach, grouped by category and listed
in English. Translations are added one batch at a time, each checked by a native
speaker before he hears it. This list is the menu; it is not a commitment to
build everything at once.

## How words are chosen

- **From his day.** Things he can touch, see or do at home, so a parent can use
  the word with him the same day.
- **Easy to picture.** One clear image, no reading needed.
- **Common everywhere.** The word exists naturally in all five languages; no
  culture-specific items that only one language has a word for.
- **Not only objects.** Actions, describing words and everyday phrases are
  included early, because understanding speech needs more than nouns.
- **Useful for puzzles.** Opposites, colours and numbers give the thinking
  puzzles material to work with.

## Picture rules (parent decision 2026-11-17)

Full rules are in the UI/UX design, section 8. For choosing words they mean:

- **No faces.** No picture shows the face of a person or an animal. Animals are
  painted from behind or curled asleep; people from behind, at a distance, or by
  hands only, modestly dressed. So "doll" is drawn without a face, and everyday
  words and actions are shown by hands and bodies, not faces.
- **Body parts one at a time.** A nose, an eye, an ear, a hand — never a whole
  face or body.
- **Taught at home.** Family words (category 8) and feelings words (category 16)
  cannot be shown under these rules, so the parents learn and teach them
  themselves. They stay on this list as the family's own menu and are not built
  into the app.
- **No instruments.** "Drum" was replaced by "bucket" (designer's pick; swap it
  for any toy that has an everyday word in all five languages).
- **Food and drink** on the list are halal; eating and drinking pictures use the
  right hand.

## Pace

Two or three new word-and-language pairs per language block is about 25 new
pairs a week. One category of 8 concepts in five languages is 40 pairs, so a new
category roughly every two weeks. With 16 categories in the app (family and
feelings words are taught at home), that is about 32 weeks, or seven to eight
months, if nothing holds it up. In practice it will take longer, because each
batch waits for its native-speaker check, its recordings and its pictures.
Slower is fine.

## Questions for the native-speaker check

Each batch goes to the checker with these, because the answer differs by
language:

- **Spanish:** say the bare word ("manzana") or with its article ("la
  manzana")?
- **Japanese:** numbers have more than one counting form; which is natural for
  a small child? Is the plain or the polite everyday phrase used? How would a
  children's book write each word: hiragana for everyday words, katakana for
  borrowed ones such as cup and ball?
- **Arabic:** which form is shared by formal and spoken Arabic; where they
  differ, which does the family want? Give each word's spelling with its vowel
  marks, because the app shows them.
- **Tamil:** the form the parents actually say at home.
- **All:** any word in the batch that a child of five would not normally hear in
  that language; replace it rather than force it.

## Not in the bank

Abstract words, brand names, screen and device words, and anything that cannot
be shown in a single calm picture or short scene.
